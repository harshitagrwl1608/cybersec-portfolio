# Digital Forensics and E-Discovery
**Source provenance:** Handwritten lecture notes from Professor Messer’s free CompTIA Security+ **SY0-701** training course.
**Professor Messer course alignment:** 4.8 – Incident Response → Digital Forensics / E-Discovery
**Coverage:** source pages 84–86

## Digital forensics
Digital forensics collects and protects information related to an intrusion so it can be analyzed. The notes mention NIST guidance for evidence collection and handling and emphasize standard practices, acquisition, analysis, reporting, and preserving evidence.

## Legal hold
A legal hold is used to preserve relevant information for legal proceedings. The notes describe:
- Preparing for litigation.
- Initiation by legal counsel.
- Identifying relevant electronically stored information.
- Defining the required data sources/types.
- Preserving data that might otherwise be altered or deleted.

## Chain of custody
Evidence integrity must be maintained.

Document:
- Who handled the evidence.
- When and how it was transferred.
- Digital signatures/logs where applicable.
- How and where data is stored.
- Who accessed it.
- What changed, if anything.
- Labels/cataloging for every item.

The objective is to demonstrate that the evidence remained authentic and controlled.

## Acquisition
Obtain data from appropriate evidence sources such as disks, logs, backups, and other relevant systems. The notes emphasize obtaining/copying data while preserving integrity for future use.

## Reporting
Document:
- How evidence was acquired/stored for legal purposes.
- Summary information.
- Detailed explanation.
- What analysis found.
- Conclusions and how findings correlate with each other.

## Preservation
Store evidence safely and maintain appropriate copies. The notes stress that preservation is critical because evidence can become inaccessible or change.

## E-discovery
E-discovery electronically collects, prepares, reviews, analyzes, interprets, or produces documents/data for a third party (often in legal processes). Results can be handed to digital-forensics/legal professionals for deeper analysis. The notes give storage-drive analysis as an example.
