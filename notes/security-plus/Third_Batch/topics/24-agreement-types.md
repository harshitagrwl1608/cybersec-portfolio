# Agreement Types

**CompTIA Security+ SY0-701 | Domain 5: Supplier and governance agreements**

Written agreements define expectations, obligations, boundaries, confidentiality, deliverables, and remedies between organizations. The exact legal effect depends on the wording, jurisdiction, signatures, and surrounding circumstances.

## 1. Service Level Agreement (SLA)

A **Service Level Agreement** defines service expectations between a provider and customer. It may cover:
- Availability or uptime targets.
- Response and resolution times.
- Support hours and escalation.
- Performance or quality metrics.
- Maintenance windows.
- Reporting and measurement.
- Remedies or service credits for unmet commitments.

**Example:** an Internet service contract may specify an availability target, maximum response time to an outage, and how quickly a technician will be dispatched.

An SLA should define how performance is measured, which exclusions apply, and what evidence is used to verify the result.

## 2. Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

A **Memorandum of Understanding** records the parties' understanding of broad terms, intent, goals, or a proposed collaboration. It is often used to align expectations before detailed implementation.

An MOU may or may not create legally binding obligations depending on its wording and applicable law. It should not be treated as automatically nonbinding.

## 3. Memorandum of Agreement (MOA)

A **Memorandum of Agreement** usually describes agreed objectives, responsibilities, or actions to be carried out by the parties. It may follow an MOU or provide a more defined statement of commitment. As with an MOU, legal enforceability depends on its text and governing law rather than the label alone.

## 4. Master Service Agreement (MSA)

A **Master Service Agreement** establishes an overarching legal framework for an ongoing commercial relationship. It can define:
- General terms and responsibilities.
- Payment and billing rules.
- Confidentiality and intellectual property.
- Security requirements and data handling.
- Liability, warranties, and dispute resolution.
- How later statements of work will be created.

Specific projects can then be added under the same MSA.

## 5. Statement of Work (SOW)

A **Statement of Work** defines a specific project or service to be delivered. It commonly includes:
- Scope and deliverables.
- Milestones, dates, and schedules.
- Roles and dependencies.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Required environments or resources.
- Fees or payment milestones.
- Reporting and change-control procedures.

An SOW is often used with an MSA. It describes the work to be done in enough detail for both parties to determine whether it was completed properly.

## 6. Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)

A **Non-Disclosure Agreement** establishes obligations to protect confidential information shared between parties. It may cover trade secrets, business plans, product details, customer information, or other non-public data.

- **Unilateral NDA:** one party primarily discloses confidential information and the other agrees to protect it.
- **Mutual/bilateral NDA:** both parties may disclose confidential information and both accept confidentiality obligations.

An NDA should define confidential information, permitted use, exclusions, duration, required safeguards, and return or destruction obligations. An NDA does not replace technical access controls.

## 7. Business Partners Agreement (BPA)

A **Business Partners Agreement** sets terms for organizations working together. It may identify:
- The parties and areas of cooperation.
- Financial and resource responsibilities.
- Decision-making authority and roles.
- Shared data and security expectations.
- Contingency planning and disaster recovery.
- How disputes, changes, and termination are handled.

The organization should document what has been agreed to rather than relying on informal assumptions.

## Choosing the appropriate agreement

| Need | Typical agreement |
|---|---|
| Service metrics and support levels | SLA |
| Broad intent or proposed cooperation | MOU |
| Defined commitments and shared objectives | MOA |
| Overarching terms for a relationship | MSA |
| Detailed project scope and acceptance | SOW |
| Protect confidential information | NDA |
| Joint business relationship and responsibilities | BPA |

Security teams should ensure agreements address access, encryption, incident reporting, audit/evidence rights, subcontractors, data return/deletion, and termination when relevant.
