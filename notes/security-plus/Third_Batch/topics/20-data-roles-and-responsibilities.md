# Data Roles and Responsibilities

**CompTIA Security+ SY0-701 | Data governance and privacy**

Organizations need explicit responsibility for the information they collect, create, store, use, share, and destroy. Data responsibilities are often business responsibilities supported by technology; they should not be assigned only to IT by default.

## 1. Data owner

A **data owner** is the business role accountable for a particular data set or information asset. The owner may:
- Classify the information.
- Define who should access it and for what purpose.
- Approve access rules and sharing.
- Identify retention and protection requirements.
- Accept or escalate risks associated with the data.
- Ensure business requirements are represented in security decisions.

The owner is not necessarily the person who operates the database or stores the files.

## 2. Data controller

Under privacy frameworks such as the GDPR, a **data controller** determines the purposes and means of processing personal data, alone or jointly with others. It decides why and how certain personal data is processed, subject to applicable law.

## 3. Data processor

A **data processor** processes personal data on behalf of a controller under the applicable contract and instructions. A processor may be a service provider or third-party company. The controller-processor relationship should define responsibilities, security, permitted use, assistance, retention, and deletion/return of data.

The precise legal definition and duties vary by jurisdiction, so the terms should be used in their relevant legal context.

## 4. Data custodian or steward

A **data custodian** or **data steward** handles day-to-day management responsibilities, which may include:
- Maintaining data quality and accuracy.
- Applying storage, backup, and security controls.
- Maintaining appropriate labels and metadata.
- Managing access through approved processes.
- Supporting retention, recovery, and disposal.
- Documenting operational handling.

These roles work with the data owner and security team. A data custodian may operate technology without being the final authority on why the data is collected or who is entitled to use it.

## Responsibility model

| Activity | Typical accountable or responsible role |
|---|---|
| Decide business purpose and classification | Data owner; controller in the applicable privacy context |
| Decide how personal data is processed | Controller, subject to law |
| Process data on behalf of another party | Processor under instructions and agreement |
| Operate storage, backups, access mechanisms | Custodian / IT operations |
| Define security controls and monitor risk | Security function with system/data owners |
| Review compliance and evidence | Compliance, privacy, risk, and audit functions as assigned |

## Key principle

Accountability should be assigned explicitly. If ownership is unclear, access requests, incidents, retention, and deletion decisions can be delayed or handled inconsistently.
